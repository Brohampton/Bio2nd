Second Messenger Cascades

Both systems from the lectures, drawn end to end: the adenylyl cyclase route that glucagon uses, and the calcium–calmodulin route that reaches the same enzyme by a different door. Then every pathway on this exam lined up against glucagon and insulin.

1 · The adenylyl cyclase cascade

One ligand produces many active phosphorylase molecules because every tier multiplies the one above it. Three separate mechanisms switch it back off.

2 · Where those phosphates land

Three targets on this exam, and the one people reverse is the first: phosphorylating the bifunctional enzyme turns its kinase half off and its phosphatase half on, so F2,6-BP goes down.

3 · The calcium cascade

The second system in the lectures. It reaches phosphorylase kinase without PKA and without any phosphorylation at all, which is how a sprinter mobilises glycogen before a hormone could possibly arrive.

4 · The fed arm

Insulin does not use cAMP. It keeps the cascade quiet and lets a phosphatase undo everything PKA did, then changes the amount of enzyme over hours.

5 · Every pathway on this exam

Two of the five lectures have no hormonal control at all. Knowing which ones is worth as much as knowing the cascades.

6 · The layer underneath

Allosteric effectors work on their own timescale and can override whichever hormone is high. These are the ones that appear across Lectures 10, 11 and 13.

Works regardless of which hormone is high

acetyl CoAActivates pyruvate carboxylase and inhibits the PDH complex. It rises in a fast because β-oxidation outruns the liver's capacity to burn it.
AMPActivates PFK-1, inhibits fructose 1,6-bisphosphatase. Low AMP with high ATP favours making glucose.
glucose 6-phosphateActivates glycogen synthase, inhibits glycogen phosphorylase — the same reciprocal shape as the hormones, one tier down.
free glucoseInhibits glycogen phosphorylase in the liver only, which fits the liver's job of serving blood glucose.
AMP in muscleActivates glycogen phosphorylase b directly, with no phosphorylation at all. Muscle has no glucagon receptors.
Ca²⁺–calmodulinThe third route to phosphorylase kinase, drawn in figure 3. Contraction itself mobilises muscle glycogen.
NADPHA potent competitive inhibitor of G6PD. The pentose phosphate pathway idles until the NADPH/NADP⁺ ratio falls.

7 · Reading the stem

What the cue is really asking

“a high glucagon-to-insulin ratio”PKA phosphorylates the bifunctional enzyme → phosphatase domain wins → F2,6-BP falls → FBPase-1 released, PFK-1 loses its activator.
“the phosphorylated bifunctional enzyme”The FBP-2 phosphatase half is the active one. Phosphorylation picks a domain; it does not switch the protein off.
“two hours after a meal”Dephosphorylated everywhere: PFK-2 kinase domain on, F2,6-BP up, pyruvate kinase active, glycogen synthase active, phosphorylase off.
“phosphorylated glycogen enzymes”Phosphorylase ON, synthase OFF — the reverse of the intuition that phosphorylation activates everything.
“α₁-adrenergic” or “calmodulin”The calcium system, not cAMP. It ends at phosphorylase kinase without any phosphorylation, because calmodulin is its δ subunit.
“exercising muscle, no hormone mentioned”Ca²⁺–calmodulin and AMP. Muscle cannot answer glucagon and cannot release glucose.
“fructose is given instead of glucose”It enters below PFK-1, bypassing the rate-limiting step, and its uptake needs no insulin.
“cortisol”Raises PEPCK transcription alongside glucagon; insulin lowers it. Slow and transcriptional, not allosteric.

One sentence that covers most of it: phosphorylation turns the liver into an exporter — glycogen phosphorylase on, glycogen synthase off, pyruvate kinase off, F2,6-BP down and so gluconeogenesis up.
